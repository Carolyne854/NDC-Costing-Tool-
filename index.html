Estimate indicative costs, financing and cost-benefit results for climate actions. All calculations run in your browser; nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

Indicative estimates only. Costs are adjusted with a country price-level index; benefit figures in the loaded example are illustrative placeholders. Replace them with project-specific data before using results in a budget or investment decision.

1. Assumptions

Import activities and cost them for a location

Upload a concept note or theory of change (PDF) or an activity list (Excel or CSV), and choose a country or region. The tool finds the activities, matches each one to benchmark costs and prices them for that location. Files are read in your browser and never uploaded. This is rule-based and approximate: review every row before adding it.

2. Interventions

Base cost = quantity × unit cost. Use quantity 1 and enter a lump sum if you only have a budget total. Countries marked “N/A” are not price-adjusted (for example, programme management fees). Picking a benchmark sets the unit cost (then set Qty to the number of units shown in the Unit column) to the median of comparable Adaptation Fund budget lines and gives a low–high range (25th–75th percentile). Benchmarks are nominal USD from projects in 10 countries by default; choose “Kenya price level” under Assumptions to rebase them to Kenya prices (experimental).

How the numbers are calculated

1) Base cost = quantity × unit cost (a benchmark median if you pick one; its 25th–75th percentile range gives the low–high cost, assuming all items sit at the low or high end together). 2) Geo-adjusted cost = base × country cost index (Kenya = 1.00, derived from World Bank price level data). 3) Spending is phased by the chosen method. 4) CAPEX interventions incur O&M each year equal to the O&M rate × cumulative prior CAPEX, continuing for the whole evaluation period. 5) Costs and benefits are discounted to the start year. 6) Benefits ramp up linearly from their start year to the steady-state amount. NPV, benefit-cost ratio, IRR and payback follow from the discounted flows. Contingency is shown on top of geo-adjusted cost for budgeting and is not included in the cost-benefit analysis.

Importing activities: a PDF, Excel/CSV list or pasted text is read in your browser. Activities are found by rules (bullet points, numbered items, “will train…” sentences, table rows), matched to one of the 11 activity groups by keywords, and priced from the benchmark library using any quantity and unit found in the text. A cost stated in the file is used as stated and is not price-adjusted. Benchmarks marked indicative only, or needing a quantity, are left unticked. The location’s cost index is then applied; a region uses the unweighted average of its countries with data. Review every imported row.

Benchmark price basis: “Kenya price level” divides each Adaptation Fund unit cost by the cost index of its source country (World Bank 2024 price level, PPP conversion factor ÷ official exchange rate, relative to Kenya) before taking medians and percentiles. It uses a reconstructed classification of the 662 rows, not the reviewed draft taxonomy, and does not adjust for inflation.

Limitations: benchmarks come from 662 itemised Adaptation Fund budget lines in 13 projects across 10 countries, grouped into activity types and unit families by a first-pass rule set that has not yet been expert-reviewed. They are nominal USD from different years and countries. Cells with few observations or projects are marked indicative only, and activities with no stated unit are left out. Four African countries have no price-level data (Eritrea, Guinea, Somalia, South Sudan); a proxy index must be entered. Liberia’s index uses its US-dollar PPP series and should be confirmed against the World Bank source.
